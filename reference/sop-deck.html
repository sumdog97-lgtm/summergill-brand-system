Sale → Onboarding SOP02
Stage 01 / Enquiry

Every enquiry gets a straight answer, same day.

Qualify in three questions

  • What does the business do, and where?
  • What's the website situation now — none, outdated, or underperforming?
  • What do they want more of? (jobs, bookings, calls, footfall)

The honesty rule

If a website genuinely won't help them, say so and walk away. Every honest "no" becomes a referral; every dishonest "yes" becomes a refund and a bad review.

Channel discipline

WhatsApp-first, always. Initial enquiry stays in writing — it creates a record and suits the client. Offer a call once scope is clear, never as the opening move.

Red flags — decline politely

  • Wants e-commerce/marketplace complexity at brochure prices
  • Won't provide business information or photos
  • Haggling below promo pricing — the price is the price

  • summergill.co.uk link (the proof wall does the selling)
  • The one-page agreement — plain English, no legalese
  • Build invoice (stage 03) once they say yes

Agreement must state

  • Build fee + monthly amount + 12-month minimum, then 30 days' notice
  • Domain registered to the client. Always.
  • Monthly includes small content changes; bigger jobs get a fixed quote first
  • On exit: full site files + domain handed over, no fees

Stage 03 / Getting paid

No payment, no pixels.

Build invoice — by bank transfer

  • Issued by Rippers Golf Ltd t/a Summergill
  • Sequential invoice number, company reg no., registered address
  • 7-day terms · business account details on the invoice
  • No VAT line at all while unregistered (never write "VAT £0")

Deposit policy

Promo slots: 100% on agreement (low ticket, high trust). Standard rate: 50% to start, 50% at approval — before launch, never after.

Monthly — GoCardless Direct Debit

  • Mandate link sent at launch, with the handover message
  • First collection one month after launch
  • £29 / £39 subscription templates, trading name "Summergill" on statements
  • Fees ≈ 1% + 20p — monitor failed payments weekly, auto-retry handles most

Annual upfront

£290/£390 invoice by transfer — zero fees. Offer it at close; it protects cash flow and signals commitment.

Sale → Onboarding SOP05
Stage 04 / Intake

Collect everything once. Chase nothing twice.

Business facts

  • Exact trading name, address, opening hours
  • Every service + real prices (prices convert — push for them)
  • Phone numbers — confirm which is WhatsApp-capable
  • Email, socials, existing domain or domain preference

Assets

  • Photos: shopfront, work/before-afters, owner at work, logo/signage
  • Google reviews (copy the best verbatim — never edit, bracket if needed)
  • Google Business Profile access or at minimum the listing link

One source of truth per client

Everything lands in the client's site.ts data file — facts live in one place, components read from it. A price change is a one-line edit forever after.

Per-client folder (internal)

  • Signed agreement + invoices
  • Intake answers + raw assets
  • Domain/registrar details, GoCardless mandate reference
Sale → Onboarding SOP06
Stage 05 / Build standards

If it isn't portfolio-worthy, it doesn't ship.

Every build includes

  • Own private repo · Astro + Tailwind · site.ts data pattern
  • Mobile-first, verified at 375px — zero horizontal overflow
  • WhatsApp-first conversion tooling (prefilled messages, estimate/enquiry widget, sticky mobile bar where it fits)
  • LocalBusiness schema, sitemap, optimised images, og preview card, custom favicon
  • "Website by Summergill" footer credit → summergill.co.uk

Non-negotiables during build

  • noindex + robots Disallow until client approval — nothing unapproved gets indexed
  • Domain registered in the client's name from day one
  • No invented claims — every fact traceable to the client or a source
  • Real photos beat stock. Always ask before settling.

Pace

Internally a build is fast. Externally it's "live within two weeks". Never reveal the real build time — the price buys the outcome and the years behind it, not the hours.

Sale → Onboarding SOP07
Stage 06–07 / Approval & launch

Sign-off in writing, then the launch checklist — in order.

06 · Approval

  • Walk the client through on their own phone first, then desktop
  • Confirm every fact: prices, hours, phone, claims (e.g. years of experience)
  • One revision round included — capture all corrections in one list
  • Written sign-off (WhatsApp message counts). No sign-off, no launch.

07 · Handover message

Send together: live link · GoCardless mandate link · what the monthly covers · how to request changes. Launch-day goodwill is the best moment for the mandate.

07 · Launch checklist

  • DNS → Vercel (A @ 76.76.21.21 · CNAME www) · bare domain primary, www 308
  • Flip noindex off + restore robots.txt Allow
  • Google Search Console: verify, submit sitemap, request indexing · repeat in Bing
  • Meta Sharing Debugger: scrape so WhatsApp previews work from share #1
  • Update client's Google Business Profile website link
  • GoCardless mandate confirmed · first collection date set
  • Test from a phone on mobile data: every CTA, the enquiry flow, the map

Stage 08 / First 30 days

The first month decides whether they stay for years.

Week 1

  • Confirm indexing (site:domain in Google)
  • Fix anything the client spots — fast, without quibbling
  • Check first WhatsApp enquiries are landing

Week 2–4

  • Promo-slot clients: request the Google review (it's in the agreement)
  • Ask for a one-line testimonial while enthusiasm is high
  • First Direct Debit collection lands — verify it

Feed the flywheel

  • Add the build to the summergill.co.uk proof wall (3-viewport screenshots, device frames)
  • New niche? Spin up the "Websites for [trade]" spoke page
  • Decrement the promo slot counter
  • Ask: "Know anyone else who needs this?" — referral at peak satisfaction

Measure

Once analytics is added: one-line monthly note to the client — visits + WhatsApp clicks. A client who sees 31 enquiries never cancels a £29 plan.

Ongoing / The care plan

Monthly operations — what £29 actually obliges us to do.

Standing duties

  • Content change requests: acknowledged same day, done within 48h (fair-use ~30 min/month)
  • Hosting, SSL and uptime — Vercel handles it; we own noticing failures
  • Domain renewals — auto-renew on, card current, never let one lapse
  • Bigger requests: fixed quote in writing before any work

Quarterly

  • Check-in message per client — invites small changes, surfaces upsells
  • Screenshot refresh on the proof wall if sites have evolved

Monthly admin (one sitting)

  • Reconcile GoCardless payouts against the client list
  • Chase failed mandates immediately — politely, by WhatsApp
  • Log income for self-assessment · watch the £90k VAT threshold

Exit protocol (the promise kept)

  • After 12 months + 30 days' notice: export site files, transfer domain, hand over cleanly
  • No exit fees, no friction, no guilt-trip — departing clients still refer
Sale → Onboarding SOP10
Always / Business hygiene

Boring things that prevent expensive things.

Records

  • Per-client folder: agreement, invoices, intake, mandate ref
  • Sequential invoice numbering, no gaps
  • All income through the business account — never personal

Security

  • 2FA on registrar, Vercel, GitHub, GoCardless, Google
  • Client credentials in a password manager, never in chat history
  • Git is the backup — every site restorable from the repo in minutes

The promises are operational, not marketing

Four public guarantees, each with an internal duty:

  • Your domain, always → registered to the client at purchase, no exceptions
  • Flat monthly → quote before work, every time, in writing
  • Leave with everything → clean exports, graceful exits
  • Quality non-negotiable → portfolio standard or it doesn't ship

Review cadence

Revisit pricing after client #5 (promotion ends) and this SOP every quarter. Version and date every change.
